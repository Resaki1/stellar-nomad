# S3 survey ship — Blender production brief

You are the vehicle artist and technical artist for **Stellar Nomad**, a realistic browser space exploration game built with three.js, React Three Fiber, and WebGPU. Build the **S3 broad survey craft** as an editable Blender asset and an optimized game asset. Make the actual 3D model, render and inspect it, export it, and verify the export. The target is a convincing autonomous spacecraft in the game's chase camera and harsh sunlight, with a practical browser budget on a MacBook M2 Pro.

Use Blender MCP if connected. Use Blender Python for repeatable parts and exports where useful; preserve the source script if it materially helps reproduce the asset. If MCP is unavailable, use a locally available Blender executable or another authorized Blender workflow. Verify you can create, save, render, and export before committing to a workflow. If you cannot run Blender, report the blocker and what you actually produced. Do not claim an export, render, or runtime test you have not performed.

## References and design authority

Read the attached images before modeling. In the repo they live beside this brief under `docs/concepts/2026-11-16/model-references/`.

1. `s3-four-view-reference.png`: source for the top outline, front cross section, side thickness and rear engine placement. Take planform from TOP; use FRONT and SIDE to constrain thickness. The drawings are concept art, so don't trace independent panels into incompatible parts.
2. `s3-bottom-reference.png`: guide for the layout of the two ventral utility bays, central keel, pipe recesses and mining optic. The projected nose face and shoulder details are illustrative; resolve their visibility from real geometry.
3. `s3-three-quarter-reference.png`: guide to depth, surface breaks and how the dorsal and ventral layouts meet. If an oblique view disagrees with the base envelope, keep the base envelope.

Build **one coherent, bilaterally symmetric vehicle**. Preserve the stocky, shallow, faceted shield-shaped hull, blunt projecting three-lens sensor nose, two broad dark swept radiator panels with clipped tips, shallow ventral equipment bays, and two raised aft shoulder nacelles with circular recessed exhausts. Give the panels credible thickness and an evident connection into the hull. Let the aft spine, braces and nacelle mounts read as load-bearing structure. Use some larger clean surfaces between denser mechanical regions. Don't replicate every generated screw or seam. No canopy, crew windows, landing gear, weapons, additional engines, giant vents, or text, serial numbers, graphics, logos or decals with lettering anywhere on the model. Keep the two nozzles dark with engines idle. The belly optic is a tool, visually distinct from a third engine.

## Scale, construction and game handoff

- Author in metres. If a project owner has specified S3 dimensions, use those. Otherwise use an **8.0 m long × 10.6 m wide × 1.7 m high blockout** as a provisional art target, including fixed appendages. These are not established gameplay dimensions. Report the actual exported bounds and intended origin. Maintain a stable root on the ship centerline near the center of the core, independent of projecting detail; preserve it in every export. After GLB export, verify **+Z nose, +Y up, +X port (ship-left)**. Use positive unit scales for the runtime asset and no unresolved mirror transforms.
- Block out the central hull, fins, shoulder nacelles, front optics, and ventral modules first. Render the same blockout from six axis-aligned orthographic cameras plus front/rear obliques above and below. Compare outlines and proportions before detailing. Keep the major silhouette landmarks aligned across views. Check a small on-screen version from the chase angle, where the two nozzles and swept radiator silhouette matter most.
- Model forms that change silhouette, produce readable specular highlights, or need real depth: hull bevels, panel steps, fins, intake/recess lips, lens sockets and exhaust throats. Bake small fasteners, shallow panel grooves, fine cooling channels and wear into maps. Use bevel widths that make sense at scale, clean normals, and deliberate hard edges. Check for shading artifacts, z-fighting, paper-thin edges and mirrored tangent-map seams. Avoid procedural patterns that repeat loudly over the entire ship.
- Keep a logical editable hierarchy in the `.blend` (hull, radiators, propulsion, avionics and ventral systems). Merge or batch the runtime static pieces by material when sensible; many objects, slots and tiny separately drawn details are unnecessary. Keep fins and other assemblies static unless asked to animate them. Do not invent a deployment rig or fabrication animation.
- Add named sockets `socket_engine_port`, `socket_engine_starboard`, and `socket_mining`. Place the two engine sockets on the nozzle centers at the exhaust exits; document nozzle diameters and thrust effect direction (aft, −Z). Place the mining socket at the forward belly tool, with a documented clear ray; ensure a forward-facing aim implementation can clear the hull, or call out where logic must be adapted. Provide exported root-relative positions and direction vectors in a handoff manifest, even if the GLB retains the empties. Do not assume the game discovers nodes by name.
- Supply simple collision proxy geometry separately from the render asset, in the same units and origin. Include a conservative low-point central convex proxy. If the fins need physical contact, provide separate simple fin hulls as candidates for a future compound collider and show their coverage in a preview. One convex envelope across the entire ship can close gaps the pilot sees. No collision for individual pipes, screws or decals.

## Materials and runtime cost

- Use glTF metallic-roughness PBR. Give warm ivory ceramic or coated armor a dielectric response, exposed metal a metallic response, graphite radiators a restrained matte finish, and yellow safety accents a small share of the surface. Distinguish materials through roughness and believable edge treatment. Use subtle service wear and occasional impact pitting, with no baked sun, studio reflections, bloom, or directional shadows in the base color. Use opaque lenses with convincing curvature and reflections by default; keep costly transparency out of the baseline export.
- Bake procedural Blender materials down to portable maps. Base color is sRGB; packed ORM is linear data (occlusion R, roughness G, metallic B); tangent-space normals are non-color, OpenGL +Y. Use enough UV padding for mipmaps and consistent detail scale. Keep physical emission roles separate: supply masks or manifest entries for small indicators and both nozzles. Ship the default export with engines unlit. Runtime exhaust, emission brightness and pre-exposure belong to the game. Don't add point lights or baked glows to make the model read in screenshots.
- Use these **starting targets**, not proof of performance: **40–60k exported triangles at LOD0**, **15–25k at LOD1**, **3–6k at LOD2**, **2–4 material slots**, preferably **about 6 or fewer exported render primitives per LOD**. Start with a shared **2K PBR texture set**; justify extra maps or 4K with a visible improvement at the actual camera distance. Measure final vertices as well as triangles; seams/material boundaries can split vertices. Preserve the fins, silhouette, nozzle rims and color blocks at each LOD; simplify hidden shapes and microdetail early. Export same bounds/origin/sockets across LODs where practical. Keep conventional core glTF materials and a plain portable GLB for bring-up. Treat KTX2, Meshopt/Draco and other optional compression as separate candidates requiring loader verification. The planet KTX2 path does not establish embedded GLB support.

## Workflow and review gates

1. **Silhouette gate.** Make a metre-scale blockout. Render against the supplied angles with the same scale and inspect the mirrored sides, gaps, front sensor, belly profile, clipped radiator tips and nozzle spacing. Correct the blockout before spending time on materials. Make routine choices yourself; record decisions where the art disagrees.
2. **Surface gate.** Detail the approved-by-reference blockout. Inspect renders under a hard directional key with restrained fill, then under subdued indirect light. Look for blown or crushed texture values, unintentional openings, stretched UVs, odd smoothing, minuscule bevels that vanish, and a noisy aft view. Keep lighting out of the asset. Check both upper and lower surfaces; deliver a true six-view sheet rendered from the actual model so future references agree.
3. **Export gate.** Export and reimport LOD0/1/2 in a clean scene (or validate with independent glTF tooling plus an actual reimport where possible). Check bounds, axes, scale, normals, texture/material bindings, default dark exhaust, socket transforms and collider fit. Inspect front, port, starboard, rear, top, bottom and oblique renders of the reimported LOD0. Check LODs together at decreasing projected sizes for silhouette loss and obvious popping. Give measured per-LOD triangle and vertex counts, render primitives, material count, image formats, texture sizes, and file size. Fix issues before handing over.
4. **Runtime boundary.** This task authorizes asset work only. Put outputs in a new `art/ships/s3/` folder (or a destination named by the user). Leave the existing ship GLB, loader, tuning, physics, scene lighting and gameplay files unchanged. If working in the repo, read applicable instructions and consult `src/components/models/ships/ShipOne.tsx`, `src/components/Spaceship.tsx`, `docs/LIGHTING_PLAN.md`, and `docs/PERF_MEASUREMENT.md` as needed. The old ship wrapper uses a particular mesh/material, legacy scale and mesh-based collision; this asset needs a deliberate later integration. Note any camera, physics, mining or VFX changes needed. When game integration is authorized, compare against the existing ship in matched scenes, resolution, DPR and quality (sunward, shadowed, chase, mining close-up, multiple ships if applicable), recording CPU/GPU times and draw calls. No Blender render alone establishes M2 Pro browser performance.

Deliver the editable `.blend` with packed or relative dependencies, portable LOD0/1/2 GLBs, separate collision proxy GLB, source/baked maps as needed, a six-view review sheet and oblique previews, and a short asset manifest with dimensions, sockets, materials, budgets, any required glTF extensions and verification results. Include paths and any unresolved tradeoffs. A clean simplified part is better than illogical filler; the ship should look manufactured, serviced and recognizably S3 at a glance.
